# King Doe methods: callable workflows from the corpus

Each workflow is distilled from the cited transcripts (video id + ~1-minute block).
Use them as reasoning tools: run the steps, ask the questions, produce the artifact.
Sources are auto-generated captions of Chris Do's public teaching; heuristics, not
laws. This directory holds notes only: fetch source captions from the video URLs in video-index.json or TOPIC-INDEX.md.

## W1. Discovery opener (run at the start of any sales or council conversation)

1. Ask the FIRST question before presenting anything: "What do you want?" or
   "What's on your mind?" (67J7070mwFg [00:00], [05:20])
2. Do the business part before the personal part if time is tight. (67J7070mwFg [07:26])
3. Listen for feelings, not specs: "What would it mean to you to...?" (67J7070mwFg [14:43])
Output: the buyer's want in their own words, verbatim.

## W2. Story Sells grid (structure any discovery or debrief)

1. Draw 3 columns: PAST | PRESENT | FUTURE. (67J7070mwFg [26:07])
2. Rows: WANT (what they desire), OBSTACLE (what blocks it), INSIGHT (what it means).
3. While the prospect talks, sort each statement into a cell.
4. Check coverage: all-past = no current action; all-future = no grounding story;
   ask into the empty columns. (67J7070mwFg [29:17], [30:20])
Output: a filled 3x3 grid; the thin column is where the next question goes.

## W3. Outcome reframing (turn any deliverable into the thing actually bought)

1. Name the deliverable (video, logo, site, plan).
2. Ask: what does the buyer get to feel/do/prove when it works? ("You don't make
   videos, you capture memories." 67J7070mwFg [24:01])
3. Find the buyer's ICP, label their pain, build the offer to match.
4. Rewrite the pitch leading with the outcome; the deliverable becomes the vehicle.
Output: one-sentence outcome offer + the deliverable as proof vehicle.

## W4. Value pricing sequence (price the client, not the job)

Prerequisite: a real decision maker with stake in the outcome — never a
subcontracted middleman. (b0fQyaBSSI0 [15:46])
1. OUTCOME: "What outcome or result are you looking for?" (b0fQyaBSSI0 [18:54])
2. METRICS: "How will you measure success?" Make it tangible, even for
   qualitative goals. (b0fQyaBSSI0 [19:59])
3. VALUE: "If this works, what is it worth to the business?" Assign a number together.
4. PRICE against that value; present the invisible work (research, positioning,
   risk) that justifies it. (b0fQyaBSSI0 [13:40])
5. Never quote before context exists: absent context, nothing is too expensive or
   too cheap. (b0fQyaBSSI0 [18:54])
Output: a price anchored to the client's own success value.

## W5. Objection clearing (before answering any single objection)

1. Surface all of them first: "Do you know what the objections are? The biggest tip
   is to know what the objections are." (-_sTrDwoZyM [00:00])
2. Confirm commitment criteria: "What has to happen for this to move forward?"
   (yyZ-d8wWJc4 [13:47])
3. Resolve concretely: budget -> installments (67J7070mwFg [18:51]); delay fear ->
   built-in contingency + fairness promise (67J7070mwFg [24:01]); "talk to my
   husband" -> confirm the spouse supports their decisions, reframe must vs need,
   offer to move forward today (67J7070mwFg [14:43]).
4. Ask for the commitment explicitly, in if-then form. (67J7070mwFg [17:49])
Output: every objection named, each with a concrete resolution, and a clear ask.

## W6. Budget honesty (get the real number early)

1. Ask directly; if you sense evasion, say: "I don't want to find you something you
   can't afford. I represent YOU and I will not settle until I find the best deal."
   (t_Zqh7EflhI [08:16])
2. Frame the budget as the tool that lets you fight for them, not a ceiling to hit.
Output: the real budget, or a stated range with the honesty frame acknowledged.

## W7. Proof engine (close the imagination gap)

1. List what a skeptic cannot imagine about your work. (FFPaVFBEIhI [08:20])
2. Document proof in every form: photos, video, testimonials, audio.
3. If proof does not exist, do one cheaper project explicitly to create it.
4. Publish the proof where buyers already look.
Output: a proof asset list tied to each claim you want believed.

## W8. Before-after story arc (any content, pitch or case study)

1. Write the BEFORE: who they were, what they feared, what they were told they
   could not do. (FFPaVFBEIhI [11:28])
2. Write the AFTER: the transformation.
3. Pull the two points apart until you find the dramatic node; set up motivation
   before showing change. (FFPaVFBEIhI [13:35])
4. Facts tell, stories sell: sensory detail and dialogue over summary. (RCocxOMQD3M [10:33])
Output: a 5-beat arc (before, stakes, turn, after, invitation).

## W9. Client triage with PVP (quarterly, or when capacity is tight)

1. List current and past clients.
2. Score each 1-10: Personal fulfillment, Value to marketplace, Profit.
   (67J7070mwFg [42:58])
3. Keep/clone the 8+ scorers; design the exit for the bottom tier.
Output: ranked client list + one niche to concentrate on (see specialization,
DK9DGsc3eaY [13:42]).

## W10. Middle-manager vs decision-maker pitch

1. Identify which one is in the room.
2. Decision maker: sell the outcome and its value (W3, W4).
3. Middle manager: sell what makes them look good and safe upstairs — on-time
   delivery, firm pricing, contingency built in, no nickel-and-diming.
   (67J7070mwFg [24:01])
Output: one pitch, correctly aimed.
